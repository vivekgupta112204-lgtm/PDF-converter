The Ultimate Guide to Going Paperless

By PDF Converter Team • 9 min read

The concept of the "paperless office" was first predicted in a Business Week article in 1975. Decades later, we are finally seeing it become a reality.

Going paperless isn't just about saving trees—though that is a massive benefit. It is about speed, efficiency, and security. Digital documents don't burn in fires, don't get lost in coffee shops, and can be searched in milliseconds.

Why Switch Now?

  • Cost Savings: The average business spends thousands annually on paper, toner, and file storage.
  • Remote Work: You can't fax a physical paper to a remote employee easily. PDFs can be emailed instantly.
  • Security: Physical files can be stolen. Encrypted PDFs are much harder to crack.

Step 1: The Digitization Phase

The first step is dealing with the backlog. You likely have boxes of old tax returns, receipts, and photos. You don't need a heavy office scanner for this.

Use your smartphone. Modern cameras are high-resolution scanners. Take photos of your documents, then use our Image to PDF tool to convert them into standardized, archive-ready files.

Step 2: Storage and Organization

Once digitized, where do they go? We recommend the 3-2-1 Backup Strategy:

  • 3 Copies of your data
  • 2 Different media types (e.g., Local Hard Drive + Cloud Storage)
  • 1 Copy offsite (Cloud covers this)

Step 3: Stopping the Inflow

Digitizing old files is useless if you keep receiving new paper.

  • Switch all bank statements to "e-delivery".
  • Opt-out of junk mail catalogs.
  • Tell clients you prefer digital invoicing.

Conclusion

Going paperless is a journey, not a destination. Start small—convert your receipts from this month into PDFs. Once you see how easy it is to find a digital file compared to rummaging through a shoebox, you'll never look back.
