How to Compress PDF Files

By PDF Converter Team • 5 min read

"File too large." It is the most frustrating error message on the internet. Whether you are trying to email a report (usually capped at 25MB) or upload a resume to a job portal (often capped at 5MB), large PDF files are a headache.

Why are PDFs so big? Usually, it's because of high-resolution images or unoptimized fonts. Here is how to shrink them down.

Method 1: Optimize Before Converting

If you are creating a PDF from images (JPG/PNG), the size of the original images dictates the size of the PDF.

Before using our JPG to PDF Converter, consider if you really need that 4K resolution photo of a receipt.

  • Crop: Remove the table surface visible in the background of your scan.
  • Resize: A standard A4 page doesn't need more than 2000px width for crystal clear printing.

Method 2: Use Online Compressors

PDF Compression tools work by removing metadata and slightly reducing the DPI (dots per inch) of images inside the document. A good compressor can reduce file size by up to 80% with almost no visible loss in quality on screen.

Method 3: "Print to PDF" Again

This is a quick hack. Open your large PDF in a browser (Chrome or Edge). Click "Print", but select "Save as PDF" as the destination. Sometimes, the browser's PDF engine will rewrite the file more efficiently than the original creator, stripping out bloat.

Understanding DPI

300 DPI: Required for professional high-quality physical printing. Keep this if you are printing a brochure.
72-150 DPI: Perfect for screen viewing and emailing. This is the sweet spot for compression.

Conclusion

Don't let file limits stop you. By managing your source images and using smart compression techniques, you can keep your documents lightweight and shareable.
